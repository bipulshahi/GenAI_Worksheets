### **Exercise 1 — Handling Missing Values**

**The Scenario:** You are the Fleet Manager. You need to guess the Odometer reading (KM) for a truck where the data is missing. If your guess is too high, you waste money on unnecessary servicing. If it's too low, the truck might break down.

* The Data (Current Fleet):

   - Truck A: 20,000 KM
   - Truck B: 25,000 KM
   - Truck C: 22,000 KM
   - Truck D (The Outlier): 1,000,000 KM (This is an old truck from a different branch)
   - Truck E (New): NULL (Missing)
 
  **Task 1:** The Calculation TestPerform these two simple math operations to find the "best guess" for
  **Truck E:**
     - The Mean (Average): Add all values (20k + 25k + 22k + 1,000k) and divide by 4.
         - Calculation: 1,067,000 ÷ 4Result: 266,750 KM
     - The Median (Middle Value): Sort the numbers and pick the middle.
         - Sorted: 20,000 — 22,000 — 25,000 — 1,000,000
         - Result: 22,000 KM (Average of the two middle values is roughly 23,500 KM)
      
   **Task 2:** Critical ThinkingWhich guess is more "representative" of the typical truck in this fleet?
           [ ] The Mean (266,750 KM)
           [ ] The Median (~23,500 KM)
    - Why did the Mean fail?
       - The single "Outlier" (the 1-million KM truck) pulled the average so high that the result no longer looks like any of the other trucks.
       
   **Task 3:** Strategy SelectionBased on the experiment above, tick the best strategy for these situations:

| If your data…                                             | Best Strategy             | Reason                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Is fairly consistent (no huge jumps)                      | [ ] Mean   [ ] Median     | Use the standard average since values are evenly distributed.        |
| Has extreme outliers (one very large or very small value) | [ ] Mean   [ ] Median     | The middle value is not affected by extreme (“freak”) numbers.       |
| Is categorical (e.g., *City* name)                        | [ ] Mode   [ ] Mean       | Text categories cannot be averaged; the most frequent value is used. |
| Is the **Target Label** (the value to be predicted)       | [ ] Drop Row   [ ] Median | Never guess the final answer the model is trying to learn.           |


Choose the **most appropriate strategy for Patient data**:

### **Exercise 3 — Data Encoding**

**The Scenario:*** You are trying to calculate the "Priority Score" for a patient. Your formula is:
    * Priority Score = (Age × 0.5) + City
    
    * **Task 1:** The Calculation Trap Try to calculate the score for this patient:
        - Patient: Age 40, City: "Delhi"
        - Calculation: (40 × 0.5) + "Delhi" = ?
        
    * **Q:** Can you complete the math? 
        - [ ] Yes
        - [ ] No, because you cannot add a number to a word.

**The Manual Encoding Fix** To fix this, we assign a "Numerical Identity" to each city:
    * Delhi = 1
    * Mumbai = 2
    * Chennai = 3
    
    * Now, recalculate for the same patient (Age 40, City: Delhi):
        - Calculation: (40 × 0.5) + 1 = _______________ (Your Result)

**The "One-Hot" Problem** If we use 1, 2, and 3, the computer might think Mumbai (2) is "twice as much" as Delhi (1). To prevent the computer from thinking one city is "greater" than another, we use One-Hot Encoding.

    - Look at the table below: 
    
    | City    | Is_Delhi? | Is_Mumbai? | Is_Chennai? | 
    | --------| --------- | ---------- | ----------- | 
    | Delhi   |    1      |    0       |     0       | 
    | Mumbai  |    0      |    1       |     0       | 
    | Chennai |    0      |    0       |     1       |

In this new table, is any city "mathematically larger" than the others? 

--

**Conclusion:** Encoding is required because:

    - Computers are Calculators: They only understand numbers (0s and 1s).
    - Neutrality: One-Hot Encoding ensures the model doesn't assume a "ranking" where one category is better than another.
